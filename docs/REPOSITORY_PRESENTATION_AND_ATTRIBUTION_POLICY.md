# Jireh Financial Aid — Repository Presentation and Attribution Policy

**Decision authority:** Jireh product owner (`@Arete0920`)  
**Owner direction recorded:** November 23, 2026  
**Scope:** `Arete0920/Jirehfinancialaid` only  
**Status:** Approved policy direction; source compliance and automated enforcement must be separately verified.

## 1. Governing principle

Present Jireh as an independently engineered financial-aid product, evaluated on its actual capabilities, source quality, security, test evidence, documentation, and human accountability. Software-development assistants, AI systems, and other automation may be used under owner direction. Tool use neither defines the product's identity nor transfers product ownership or release authority.

Avoid unnecessary AI, model, assistant, vendor, or tool branding in public and maintained first-party repository language. **This is a presentation rule, not permission to conceal facts or falsify provenance.** Do not claim the code is entirely human-written, that assistance was never used, or that an automated review is independent human approval when those claims are not established.

## 2. Covered surfaces

Apply the standard prospectively to material created or materially edited in:

- Repository description, topics, landing/readme pages, product documentation, screenshots/captions, public release notes, and buyer-facing technical materials.
- First-party source comments, examples, sample configurations, issue and PR templates, and maintained contributor or handoff guidance.
- New or edited pull-request titles and descriptions, and first-party issue titles/descriptions when used as current project guidance.
- Product interfaces, demonstration labels, external descriptions, and generated summaries published by the project.

Describe what the software **does**, what is **implemented**, how it was **verified**, and who **authorizes** release. Prefer precise terms such as validation, document review, deterministic calculation, risk analysis, forecast, or decision support *only where technically accurate*. Do not imply an unimplemented feature exists.

## 3. Prohibited presentation and attribution

- No promotional "AI-built", "AI-developed", "generated by [tool]", "autonomous engineer", or vendor/tool identity labels for Jireh or its maintainers.
- Do not attribute product ownership, architecture authority, acceptance, security sign-off, or release decisions to a development assistant or workflow.
- Do not present a bot, code-generation tool, static analyzer, or passing CI job as a human reviewer or as certification of production readiness.
- Do not add unearned human co-author credits, fabricated review identities, unsupported provenance, or claims of independent human approval.
- Do not add model/provider names to ordinary first-party documentation or PR narratives solely to explain how text or code was drafted.

These restrictions do **not** prohibit using development tools, citing genuine third-party dependencies, or discussing real technical functionality when necessary.

## 4. Required factual exceptions and preservation

Preserve truth even where a reference is not presentation-neutral:

- Immutable Git authors, co-authors, signed commits, original PR/issue history, historical branches, audit records, and authentic provenance must not be rewritten to disguise past activity.
- Required license, copyright, dependency, security, and contributor attributions must remain intact.
- A real AI/model integration, external data processor, contractual disclosure, privacy implication, or customer-facing automated decision must be accurately documented where relevant. Do not hide an active dependency or compliance obligation.
- Technical governance documents, including **this policy**, may explicitly identify assisted-development methods when necessary to state the control.
- Any narrow exception affecting maintained first-party language requires a recorded reason, supporting evidence, and owner review. Broad or silent exclusions are not permitted.

Neutral wording must never erase a capability, algorithm, service relationship, legal obligation, or risk. Historical source should be referenced honestly, not retroactively sanitized.

## 5. Human decision and review authority

The Jireh product owner controls product requirements, technical direction, scope, material acceptance, and release authorization. Contributors receive evidence-backed credit for identifiable substantive work; tool use does not automatically make a tool a product author.

The solo-owner review path must say when independent human review is **unavailable**. Automated tests, scans, assisted review, and exact-commit evidence can support a documented owner decision; they must not be labeled independent human approval. This policy grants no deployment, payments, customer-data, integration, or production release authorization.

## 6. Enforcement standard

**Required target controls (not presumed implemented):**

1. Review tracked first-party filenames, text, and maintainable media metadata for prohibited promotional attribution and provider/method branding; use contextual review to avoid treating harmless substrings or legitimate technical references as violations.
2. Check new/edited PR titles and descriptions, not just source files. Audit repository description/topics and other external surfaces separately where CI cannot read them.
3. Require narrow, documented exceptions for legally required attribution, historical evidence, necessary technical disclosure, and this policy. Never delete required disclosure to make a check green.
4. Tie any enforcement result to the exact branch/PR SHA. A clean source scan does not establish history cleanliness, independent approval, security certification, or production readiness.
5. Record baseline findings and remediations with the file or metadata location, disposition, and test evidence. Do not bulk-rewrite historical commits, merged discussions, or branches.

**Implementation truth:** Merely merging this policy does not install a CI gate or certify compliance. Until enforcement is implemented and verified, reviews must explicitly identify the manual-only control and unresolved findings.

### Operational review and exception records

- Inspect tracked Git objects rather than relying on a filename-extension list. A media, nontext, link or submodule object requires an exact-object review record, including text-based media whose visual meaning cannot be established by a text scan.
- The three named governance/control files are content exceptions because they define and test the vocabulary; their filenames and object types remain checked. Review changes to those controls explicitly. Dependency and vendor paths are not blanket content exceptions.
- Record necessary factual references by exact location, line, matched term and full-line digest in the gate's reviewed-reference registry. Record assets by path and exact Git object, or uploaded release assets by release ID, asset ID and exact SHA-256 digest. Each record must cite a factual reason and actual owner decision. A changed reference or object needs renewed review. Never manufacture an approval to pass CI.
- The required foundation job continues to enforce tracked content and applicable pull-request narratives. The separate presentation workflow checks repository name, description, topics and homepage, and applicable first-party issue or published release narratives. Its issue/release results are alerts, not branch-protection or prepublication approvals. Owner-adopted external issue guidance must also be reviewed.
- Before publishing or materially editing a release, screenshot, external description, presentation, document or buyer-facing material, inventory the actual artifact and its version; review wording, visual content, metadata, embedded third-party attribution and any material technical or contractual disclosure. Record the reviewed version, reviewer, date, findings, disposition and owner decision in the maintained project record. Re-run the repository presentation workflow on metadata changes that do not otherwise trigger it. Do not treat a post-publication check as approval to publish.
- Do not claim comprehensive compliance if an external artifact inventory, a relevant contextual claim, an owner decision or exact-version review is missing. The owner validates approval references; a machine cannot establish an independent human approval from text.
